WHY IT APPEARS AS A BOOST FREQUENCY

  qcom_cpufreq_hw_read_lut() sets CPUFREQ_BOOST_FREQ for the last LUT_TURBO_IND
  entry itself, which is why 3360000 lands in scaling_boost_frequencies rather
  than scaling_available_frequencies, and why the non-boost ceiling
  (cpuinfo_max_freq = scaling_max_freq) stays at 2956800. That is the driver's
  own classification from the hardware LUT, and it is exactly what this change
  left alone: the board node declares no turbo-mode.

  The global boost switch reads 0 and was NOT changed:
  $ cat /sys/devices/system/cpu/cpufreq/boost   -> 0

FREQUENCY OBSERVATION — reported, but explicitly NOT a success criterion

  A brief single-thread load on cpu7 with the frequency left unpinned:

  $ cat /sys/devices/system/cpu/cpufreq/policy7/scaling_cur_freq
  1977600, then 2956800 six times over ~10 s while a cpu7-bound loop ran

  It did not reach 3360000, and that is not a failure. The prime bin is a boost
  state, boost is off (0), and thermal, current-limit and scheduler state can all
  legitimately keep a short test below the top bin. The change was NOT validated
  by observing the frequency, and the frequency was NOT forced:

    * no `echo 3360000 > scaling_min_freq`
    * no `echo 1 > /sys/devices/system/cpu/cpufreq/boost`
    * no governor change, no affinity pinning beyond a single temporary loop

  The three hard criteria are 1, 2 and 3 above, and all three are met.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW

  It does NOT show the CPU wedge is fixed. That stall is a separate,
  pre-existing failure with its own evidence in docs/CPU_WEDGE_EVIDENCE.md and a
  measured rate of 1 in 29 boots. A single clean boot says nothing about it, and
  establishing any link between this OPP and that stall would need a
  pre-registered A/B experiment of its own.
